# Competitive landscape: online marketplaces and classifieds in Saudi Arabia (as of Nov 2026)

Research note on method: about 20 web searches/fetches in English and Arabic, run 14 Nov 2026. Most findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from full reads of each page; only the Wamda 2016 Haraj article and the Semrush haraj.com.sa page were fetched in full. No app-store rating or review counts were retrieved for any app (see Gaps). Anything not labelled with a source is in "Inferences" or "Gaps".

## 1. Horizontal classifieds: Haraj, OpenSooq and others — scale, model, weaknesses

### Takeaway
Haraj is the entrenched horizontal leader (about 10M monthly web visits in Oct 2026, strongest in cars, real estate, livestock) and runs on a trust-based 1% seller commission with no in-platform payment; that missing payment/escrow/verification layer is its documented weakness and is exactly what funded verticals (Soum, Syarah) built on top of. OpenSooq is a distant second in KSA; a head-on horizontal competitor is not a realistic option for a solo developer.

### Cited Findings
- Haraj launched in 2008, founded by Yousef Al Rasheedi (KFUPM computer engineering graduate, 2007); about 30 employees as of 2016 — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- Haraj business model (2016 data): sellers pay 1% commission on each sale, buyers pay nothing; commission paid by bank transfer on an honour basis; discounts below 1% for car and real-estate sellers; subscription packages of SAR 1,490 for six months or SAR 2,390 per year for frequent advertisers — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- Haraj scale (2016 data, old): about 10,000 new offers daily, 600,000 monthly users, 150,000+ advertisers in real estate, reported monthly sales of SAR 400M in 2015, valuation SAR 600M per a Sabq report cited by Wamda — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- In March 2016 the founder refused an offer of $20M for 20% of the company — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- Haraj categories: cars are the highest-selling category, plus electronics, real estate, livestock (sheep, cows, birds) and services; separate sections per Saudi city — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- Haraj weaknesses named in 2016: "super basic" design, no online payment option (which also makes commission hard to track), acts only as an intermediary with no online purchase — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- Haraj current web traffic: 10.2M visits in Oct 2026, 10.29M in Sep 2026, 14.05M in Aug 2026; rank 28 in Saudi Arabia; ranked in the Automotive category — [Semrush, updated 11 Nov 2026](https://www.semrush.com/website/haraj.com.sa/overview/)
- Semrush lists opensooq.com (12.87M visits, apparently its all-country total) and syarah.com (893.91K visits) as Haraj's top competitors — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/haraj.com.sa/overview/)
- In Semrush's Oct 2025 Saudi automotive ranking, haraj.com.sa had 10.82M monthly visits, expatriates.com 2.83M and opensooq.com 2.55M — [Semrush top vehicles sites, Saudi Arabia](https://www.semrush.com/website/top/saudi-arabia/vehicles/)
- Haraj's FAQ says fraud and scamming are among the common reasons for account bans, and commission is still registered through a fee account on the site (no current rate found in the snippet) — [Haraj FAQ](https://haraj.com.sa/en/faq)
- A security firm report on Ramadan scams names fake point-of-sale setups registered on platforms like Haraj as an observed scam type — [Resecurity](https://www.resecurity.com/blog/article/cybercriminals-accelerate-online-scams-during-ramadan-and-eid-fitr)
- OpenSooq claims over 60 million users across 20 countries (company-wide, not Saudi-specific) — [OpenSooq app listing via AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.opensooq.OpenSooq)
- Semrush says OpenSooq's core audience is in Saudi Arabia, followed by the UAE and Iraq — [Semrush opensooq.com overview](https://cw.semrush.com/website/opensooq.com/overview)
- Expatriates.com is the third notable horizontal site by Saudi traffic in the vehicles category (2.83M visits, Oct 2025) — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/top/saudi-arabia/vehicles/)
- Newer funded horizontal-ish entrant: Rwaj, a Saudi live-shopping and auction marketplace, raised $1.2M pre-seed in Sep 2026 — [Wamda, Sep 2026](https://www.wamda.com/2026/09/rwaj-raises-1-2-million-scale-live-shopping-marketplace)

### Inferences
- Haraj's 2016 user figures (600K monthly users) are far out of date; 10M+ monthly web visits in 2026 (excluding app usage) implies a much larger base today. No current official user or listing count was found.
- Haraj appears never to have announced outside funding (it turned down $20M in 2016), which suggests it is itself the best-known Saudi example of a self-funded marketplace; the sources do not state this explicitly.
- The Haraj model (free listing, honour-system commission, no escrow, minimal verification) leaves trust gaps in any category where the item is expensive, hard to inspect, or easy to misrepresent. Every funded Saudi vertical found in this research attacks one of those gaps with inspection, payment or logistics.
- The 12.87M OpenSooq figure in Semrush is almost certainly global, since the Saudi vehicles-category figure a year earlier was 2.55M; OpenSooq's total Saudi traffic is unknown.

### Gaps
- No current (2025-2026) official Haraj figures for users, listings, GMV or the commission rate; the 1% figure is confirmed only by a 2016 article.
- No app-store rating or review counts were retrieved for Haraj or OpenSooq.
- No aggregated data on fraud complaints against Haraj or a Saudi regulator statement specific to Haraj was found; search quality and review features were not assessed from any source.
- Dubizzle/OLX-type presence in KSA and other horizontal apps (e.g. Shobbak) were not verified.

## 2. Funded vertical marketplaces in Saudi Arabia, by vertical

### Takeaway
The high-value verticals are taken by well-funded players: used cars (Syarah, $82M+), vacation rentals/istirahat (Gathern, $72M Series B at about $266M valuation), B2B wholesale (Sary/SILQ, $110M), construction materials (BRKZ, reported $31M Series B), used electronics (Soum, $18M Series A), fashion (Aya, SAR 26M). Real estate is additionally closed off by REGA licensing.

- Speero's lack of visible funding since 2021 suggests spare parts has no well-capitalised leader, but it is an operations-heavy business (warehouses, fitment data), so "weakly funded" does not mean "easy".

- Aqar's own user, traffic and funding figures were not found from a primary source.

## 3. Per-vertical verdict: dominant player, several weak players, or essentially nothing

### Takeaway
On the evidence found, verticals split three ways: locked up by funded leaders (cars, stays, B2B, construction, electronics, real estate, new fashion), crowded with small unfunded apps but no visible leader (livestock, productive families, home services, events, pets supplies), and those where the search found no dedicated funded platform at all (kids' used items, fashion resale, handicrafts, tutoring, dates/farm produce, pet animal classifieds, falcon trading outside the official club). The third group is "nothing found", which is weaker than "nothing exists".

### Inferences
- Lowest funded-competitor risk on this evidence: livestock, events/wedding services, productive families (non-food side), pets, and the unresearched group (kids' used items, fashion resale, handicrafts, tutoring). In all of these the real incumbent is Haraj plus WhatsApp/Instagram/Snapchat, not a funded startup.
- Livestock is the clearest example of "several weak players": at least six apps with overlapping features (auctions, payment, transport) and no sign that any has won. That signals real demand and also that supply-side habits (physical markets, Haraj, WhatsApp groups) are hard to shift; a new entrant needs a sharper wedge than "an app for livestock".
- Events/weddings has attracted only sub-$300K rounds in 2024-2026, both for tooling/planning rather than a vendor marketplace; a narrow vendor-booking marketplace in one city would not face a funded rival today.
- Real estate should be treated as closed for a solo developer: licensing, per-ad licence validation and platform obligations (new rules from May 2026) add compliance cost on top of 71 licensed competitors.
- Falcons are a small, high-value, club-governed market; an independent marketplace would compete with an official body, so it is low-competition but structurally unattractive.

### Gaps
- No app-store ratings, review counts or download numbers were collected for any vertical app, so "weak player" judgements for livestock, productive families and pets rest on the absence of funding news and the number of overlapping apps, not on measured traction.
- "Nothing found" for kids' used items, fashion resale, handicrafts, tutoring and dates/farm produce reflects limited search coverage (one combined query each at most); dedicated searches are needed before calling these empty.
- Excluded from the opportunity side per brief and not researched: perfumes, tourism-experience platforms, fintech, food/grocery delivery.

## 4. Failed or shut-down marketplace startups and lessons

### Takeaway
Documented Saudi closures in 2025-2026 are concentrated in delivery and general e-commerce, where subsidised competition and logistics costs killed even players with millions of customers; no shutdown of a Saudi niche classifieds marketplace was found in the search results.

### Cited Findings
- Shgardi (delivery) permanently ceased operations in Oct 2025 after six years, citing intense competition and price dumping — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1852537)
- Shgardi had served 3 million customers and 7 million orders in 35 cities, and missed its projected 2025 breakeven; HungerStation holds over 50% share in key cities — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=20093)
- Zode (e-commerce platform in Saudi Arabia) ceased operations on 1 July 2025, nine months after launch, with after-sales service until 14 July — [TMTPost, Aug 2025](https://en.tmtpost.com/news/7659703)
- KartNGo (Saudi self-service startup) ended operations in July 2026 after more than seven years, citing circumstances and challenges beyond its control — [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/news/21561485/saudi-self-service-startup-kartngo-ends-operations-after-seven-years-in-business?lang=en)
- Wadi.com (Dubai-based, Saudi-focused) shut its online marketplace after pivoting to groceries — [MENAbytes](https://www.menabytes.com/wadi-shuts-down-marketplace/)
- Awok (Dubai) shut down a year after a $30M Series A; commentary pointed to high customer acquisition costs, low unit prices and self-run logistics — [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/dubais-awok-shuts-down-a-year-after-raising-usd-30-million-series-a-round)
- Sprii (UAE, baby products) went into liquidation in Dec 2020 after failing to raise funding — [Wamda, 2020](https://www.wamda.com/2020/12/uaes-ecommerce-platform-sprii-goes-liquidation)
- Careem scaled back operations in Saudi Arabia — [Bridge MENA](https://bridgemena.com/news/careem-scales-back-in-saudi-arabia-a-strategic-reset-in-the-super-app-era)
- A Saudi commentary attributes early-stage failures to founders' weak grasp of unit economics and cash flow and to rising costs such as rent and talent — [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/experts-thoughts/15313?lang=en)

### Inferences
- Lesson 1: avoid any model that holds inventory or runs its own delivery; every documented failure carried logistics or inventory cost against better-funded rivals.
- Lesson 2: scale did not protect Shgardi (3M customers) in a commodity category with a subsidising leader; a small niche where the leader has no reason to subsidise is safer than a large one.
- Lesson 3: Sprii shows a vertical (baby products) can fail when it depends on a next funding round; a solo developer should pick a model that is cheap to run at low volume (listings, lead fees, subscriptions) and never needs a round to survive.

### Gaps
- No documented failure of a Saudi niche classifieds/C2C marketplace was found, so there is no direct evidence on why niche listing apps die (most likely they fade quietly without press coverage).
- Reasons for Zode's and KartNGo's closures are not stated in detail in the sources seen.

## 5. Small or bootstrapped niche marketplaces that succeeded

### Takeaway
The only well-evidenced Saudi marketplace that grew large without visible outside funding is Haraj itself; no documented case of a small bootstrapped niche Saudi marketplace success was found in this research.

### Cited Findings
- Haraj was built by a single technical founder from 2008, reached SAR 400M in reported monthly sales by 2015 with about 30 staff, and declined a $20M investment offer in 2016 — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- Haraj's method: free buying, 1% honour-system commission, very basic design, city-specific sections, reputation perks for sellers with 20+ positive comments a year — [Wamda, 2016](https://www.wamda.com/en/2016/05/ksa-car-seller-haraj-turns-down-20m)
- Rekaz, a Saudi SMB platform, describes using Haraj to reach its early small-business customers — [Rekaz Substack](https://rekaz.substack.com/p/hustle-heart-and-haraj-the-unfiltered)
- Taker (Saudi restaurant software, not a marketplace) bootstrapped by pre-selling five-year subscriptions to four anchor customers — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/260)
- Awok (UAE) was bootstrapped from 2013 with $30,000 and three employees before raising funding, and later shut down — [Entrepreneur ME](https://entrepreneur.com/en-ae/finance/we-got-funded-uae-based-e-commerce-enterprise-awok-raises/331894); [MENAbytes](https://www.menabytes.com/awok-shuts-down/)

### Inferences
- Haraj's playbook is the relevant one for a solo developer: listings only, no inventory or delivery, minimal product, monetise through commission or seller subscriptions, and let seller reputation substitute for platform guarantees. It was built in 2008 with little competition, so the timing is not repeatable, but the cost structure is.
- Rekaz's use of Haraj as an acquisition channel suggests a practical tactic: recruit the first sellers for a niche marketplace from the matching Haraj category, where they already advertise.
- Taker's pre-selling approach translates to marketplaces as signing a handful of paying anchor sellers (for example wedding vendors or livestock traders) before building.

### Gaps
- No verified examples of small, profitable, unfunded niche marketplaces in Saudi Arabia were found; such businesses rarely get press, so absence here is not evidence that none exist.
- Haraj's funding history after 2016 was not verified; "bootstrapped" is an inference from the refused offer and absence of funding news.
